Schiedea apokremnos H.St.John

— · cliff schiedea, Nā Pali sea-cliff schiedea

FamilyCaryophyllaceae
Scientific nameSchiedea apokremnos H.St.John
Hawaiian name(s)—
English name(s)cliff schiedea, Nā Pali sea-cliff schiedea
StatusEndemic
ConservationUS Endangered (federally listed)
Coastal zonesSea cliff
OccurrenceKauaʻi endemic. Known from a very small number of near-vertical basalt sea cliffs on the Nā Pali coast, growing in cliff-face crevices where feral goats cannot reach. One of the most restricted plants in the guide; most population points are known only from rope surveys or boat/offshore vantage. Not a plant the average hiker will encounter — included because it is a signature Nā Pali coastal endemic and a good visual anchor for cliff-face biology.
Uncertainty: Anatomy details (petal presence, exact stem length range) are drawn from published *Schiedea* revisions; individual wild plants may vary. Do NOT climb or approach any Nā Pali sea-cliff plant — a federally listed species and inherently dangerous terrain.

How to identify

Growth form
Low subshrub, often pendent from the cliff face — multiple slender, semi-woody branches trailing outward and downward from a woody rootstock wedged into a rock crevice. Total branch length on established plants can reach 0.5–1 m; the plant does not stand erect off the cliff.
Size
Individual branches to 0.5–1 m long; the plant hugs the cliff rather than standing tall.
Leaves
Narrow, linear-oblanceolate, fleshy-succulent, 2–5 cm long and only a few millimeters wide; opposite along the slender stems (Caryophyllaceae — leaves in pairs, one pair per node). Green to slightly glaucous; a hand lens shows a smooth glabrous surface without conspicuous hairs.
Flowers
Small, wind-pollinated, greenish-white, in open branched clusters (cymes) at the shoot tips. Individual flowers ~5 mm across; petals absent or minute — the visible parts are mostly the five green-white sepals and the small stamens. Unshowy but distinctive when a shoot is examined.
Fruit / seed
Small dry capsule opening at the top by three valves, releasing tiny dark seeds; not conspicuous from a distance.
Bark / stem
Slender woody stems at the base, becoming green and herbaceous toward the tips; older stems grey-brown.
Where it sits on the coast
Near-vertical to overhanging basalt sea cliffs, essentially at the sea/rock interface. Observed most reliably from a boat passing beneath Nā Pali cliffs during calm weather.

Clinchers (features that lock the ID)

  • Pendent low subshrub rooted in a cliff-face crevice — no other Kauai coastal plant grows quite this way.
  • Narrow, fleshy, opposite (paired-at-a-node) succulent leaves — the Caryophyllaceae signature at this scale.
  • Small unshowy greenish-white flowers in open clusters, wind-pollinated.

Look-alikes

  • Schiedea membranacea and other upland Schiedea — Other Kauaʻi *Schiedea* grow in higher-elevation mesic to wet forest, are usually more erect, and are found nowhere near the sea. If it is on a Nā Pali sea cliff, it is not one of those species.
  • Portulaca species (small coastal succulents) — Portulaca leaves are alternate (single at a node), fleshy- cylindrical, and its flowers are showy pink to yellow with obvious petals — the opposite of Schiedea's small greenish petal-less flowers and paired leaves.
  • Brighamia insignis (ʻālula, in this guide) — ʻĀlula is a stout single-stemmed succulent with a terminal rosette of paddle-shaped leaves — very different growth form, though it shares the sea-cliff habitat.

Ecology

A relict of Kauaʻi's near-shore cliff flora. Its sea-cliff refuge is exactly the microhabitat that feral goats and pigs cannot reach; extirpation of near-cliff-top populations by ungulates and landslide loss of individual crevices are the main ongoing threats. Wind pollination is consistent with life in an environment where insect pollinators are scarce. [1], [2], [3], [5], [48], [50]
