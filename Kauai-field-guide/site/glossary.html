Glossary

Botanical and Hawaiian terms used throughout the guide.

Endemic
Native to Hawaiʻi and found nowhere else on Earth.
Indigenous
Native to Hawaiʻi but also naturally occurring elsewhere (arrived without human help).
Polynesian introduction (canoe plant)
Plants brought to Hawaiʻi by Polynesian voyagers before European contact.
Modern introduction
Plants introduced to Hawaiʻi after European contact (post-1778).
Invasive
A non-native species that spreads aggressively and displaces native flora or alters ecosystems.
Strand
The zone of vegetated sand or coral rubble immediately above the high-tide line.
Dune
Windblown sand deposits above the strand, often stabilized by low creeping vegetation.
Sea cliff
Steep coastal rock face; on the Nā Pali coast these rise hundreds to thousands of feet and host several endemics found nowhere else.
Valley mouth
The lower, wider end of a coastal valley where it meets the shore — a distinct habitat with freshwater influence.
Riparian
Along the banks of streams; on this coast, the freshwater streams that spill onto Nā Pali beaches.
Wahi pana
A storied or sacred place. The Nā Pali coast is wahi pana in its entirety.
Kahakai
Beach or shoreline (Hawaiian).
Pali
Cliff (Hawaiian). Nā Pali = "the cliffs."
Obovate
Leaf shape shaped like an egg with the wider end away from the stem.
Lanceolate
Leaf shape long and narrow, tapering to a point like a lance.
Cordate
Heart-shaped leaf base.
Palmate
Leaflets or lobes radiating from a common point, like fingers from a palm.
Pinnate
Leaflets arranged in two rows along a central axis (like a feather).
Entire margin
A leaf edge that is smooth, without teeth or lobes.
Corymb
A flat- or round-topped flower cluster in which the outer flowers open first.
Drupe
A fleshy fruit with a hard inner stone enclosing the seed (e.g., a cherry, or kou fruit).
Rosette
A tight cluster of leaves arranged in a circle, usually near the ground or the top of a stem.
Prostrate
Growing flat along the ground.
Halophyte
A plant adapted to salty conditions — coastal strand plants are typically halophytes.
Naupaka
The Hawaiian name for the genus Scaevola; on this coast usually refers to naupaka kahakai (Scaevola taccada).
Kukui
Aleurites moluccanus, the state tree of Hawaiʻi; a Polynesian canoe plant of valley mouths.
Milo, Kou, Hala
Indigenous coastal trees — milo (Thespesia populnea), kou (Cordia subcordata), hala (Pandanus tectorius).
ʻĀlula
Brighamia insignis — the "cabbage on a stick," a Kauai Nā Pali cliff endemic listed as US Endangered.
