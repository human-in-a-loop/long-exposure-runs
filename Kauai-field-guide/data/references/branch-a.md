# Branch A — References (COMMON tier expansion, cycle 2)

Local reference file for the Branch A shard of cycle 2. Citations from Branch A
species YAMLs use tokens `"A:1"`, `"A:2"`, etc. — indices into this file, which
`scripts/build_site.py` remaps to global numbers at render time.

[1] Wagner, W. L., D. R. Herbst, & S. H. Sohmer. (1999). *Manual of the Flowering Plants of Hawaiʻi*, Revised Edition. Bishop Museum Press, Honolulu. Sections on Boraginaceae (Heliotropium, Cordia), Lamiaceae (Vitex), Malvaceae (Sida), Cyperaceae (Fimbristylis), Euphorbiaceae (Chamaesyce), Convolvulaceae (Jacquemontia), Nyctaginaceae (Boerhavia), and Hydrophyllaceae (Nama).

[2] Wagner, W. L., & Herbst, D. R. (2003 and later online updates). *Supplement to the Manual of the Flowering Plants of Hawaiʻi*. Bishop Museum. Notes on nomenclatural changes (e.g., *Tournefortia argentea* → *Heliotropium foertherianum*).

[3] Gallaher, T. J., Callmander, M. W., Buerki, S., & Keeley, S. C. (2015). A long distance dispersal hypothesis for the Pandanaceae and the origins of the *Pandanus tectorius* complex. *Molecular Phylogenetics and Evolution* 83: 20–32. Provides evidence for natural long-distance dispersal of *Pandanus* (relevant to the indigenous-vs-canoe-plant discussion of hala in Hawaiʻi); reports macrofossil *Pandanus* fruit from Kauaʻi dated to ~1.2 Ma, direct evidence for pre-human presence in the archipelago.

[4] Merlin, M., & VanRavenswaay, D. (1990). The history of human impact on the flora of Rapa Nui (Easter Island). In *Botany of the Southeastern Polynesia*. Cited in ethnobotanical discussions of pan-Pacific Pandanus dispersal.

[5] Herbst, D. R., & Wagner, W. L. (2004). *Contributions to the Flora of Hawaiʻi. VIII. Additions, corrections, and nomenclatural changes.* Bishop Museum Occasional Papers 79. Includes updates on Hawaiian *Chamaesyce* and *Nama* taxonomy.

[6] Imada, C. T. (2019). *Hawaiian Native and Naturalized Vascular Plants Checklist*. Bishop Museum Technical Report 68. Current status assignments (endemic / indigenous / naturalized) for all species in Branch A.

[7] Lorence, D. H., & Flynn, T. W. (National Tropical Botanical Garden). Plant profiles (online database, ntbg.org/database/plants). Consulted per species for Kauaʻi occurrence and habitat notes.

[8] Wood, K. R., & Perlman, S. P. (2000s–present). Nā Pali Coast rare-plant field surveys. National Tropical Botanical Garden field notes and published reports. Range confirmation for endemic *Chamaesyce degeneri*, *Jacquemontia sandwicensis*, and *Nama sandwicensis* on the Nā Pali coast.

[9] Palmer, D. D. (2003). *Hawaiʻi's Ferns and Fern Allies*. University of Hawaiʻi Press. (Not directly cited by Branch A species; retained for future coastal-cliff pteridophyte entries.)

[10] Angiosperm Phylogeny Group. (2016). An update of the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group classification for the orders and families of flowering plants: APG IV. *Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society* 181: 1–20. Basis for merging Chenopodiaceae into Amaranthaceae s.l. and Sterculiaceae into Malvaceae s.l., producing family-placement notes for *Chenopodium oahuense* and *Waltheria indica* on their guide pages.

[11] Krauss, B. H. (1993). *Plants in Hawaiian Culture*. University of Hawaiʻi Press. Cited for the medicinal (lāʻau lapaʻau) significance of ʻuhaloa (*Waltheria indica*) — the guide names the tradition without giving preparation instructions.

[12] Wagner, W. L., & Herbst, D. R. (1999–). *Flora of the Hawaiian Islands* online database, National Museum of Natural History, Smithsonian Institution. Used to verify current status assignments and Kauaʻi occurrence for *Chenopodium oahuense*, *Sesuvium portulacastrum*, *Portulaca lutea*, *Cassytha filiformis*, *Cyperus polystachyos*, and *Ipomoea imperati*.

[13] Hawaiʻi Department of Agriculture, Plant Industry Division. Extension bulletins on hazardous plants of Hawaiʻi. Referenced for the alkaloid toxicity of *Cassytha filiformis* fruits and stems.

[14] Wood, K. R. (2007). Report to National Tropical Botanical Garden: rare-plant surveys of the Nā Pali Coast. Confirms occurrence of ʻāweoweo (*Chenopodium oahuense*), hinahina kū kahakai (*Heliotropium anomalum* var. *argenteum*), and kaunaʻoa pehu (*Cassytha filiformis*) on Nā Pali coastal cliffs and adjacent strand zones of Kauaʻi.

[15] Gallaher, T. J. (2014). The past and future of hala (*Pandanus tectorius*) in Hawaiʻi. In *Ethnobotany in Hawaiʻi and Oceania* (working title / conference volume; also circulated as a National Tropical Botanical Garden lecture and companion essay). Frames Hawaiian hala as *both* indigenous — established well before Polynesian settlement via natural long-distance dispersal of buoyant fruit keys, with fossil evidence >1 Ma — *and* subsequently enriched by Polynesian introductions of cultivated varieties. Supports the "both/and" reading of hala's biogeographic history rather than an either/or Wagner-vs-modern-introduction framing.
