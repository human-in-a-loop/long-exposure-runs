# Branch B — References

Local numbering for Branch B (NOTABLE tier expansion). Cited from species
YAMLs as tokens `"B:1"`, `"B:2"`, …; the build script assigns global ids at
render time.

[1] Rock, J. F. (1913, reprinted 1974). *The Indigenous Trees of the Hawaiian Islands*. Charles E. Tuttle Company, Rutland, Vermont (reprint of 1913 Honolulu edition). Public domain.

[2] Harries, H. C. (1978). The evolution, dissemination and classification of *Cocos nucifera* L. *The Botanical Review* 44(3): 265–320. (Presents the case for pre-Polynesian pantropical drift of the coconut.)

[3] Lowry, P. P. II & Plunkett, G. M. (2010). Recircumscription of *Polyscias* (Araliaceae) to include six related genera, with a new infrageneric classification and a synopsis of species. *Plant Diversity and Evolution* 128(1–2): 55–84. (The nomenclatural transfer *Reynoldsia* → *Polyscias*.)

[4] Chinnock, R. J. (2007). *Eremophila and Allied Genera: A Monograph of the Myoporaceae*. Rosenberg Publishing, Kenthurst NSW, Australia.

[5] Herbst, D. R. (1988). Notes on the biogeography of the strand plants of the Hawaiian Islands. Contributions to the *Manual of the Flowering Plants of Hawaiʻi* (Bishop Museum Press, subsequently 1990/1999). (Argument for treating *Hibiscus tiliaceus* as indigenous.)

[6] Rubinoff, D., Holland, B. S., Shibata, A., Messing, R. H., & Wright, M. G. (2010). Rapid invasion despite lack of genetic variation in the erythrina gall wasp (*Quadrastichus erythrinae* Kim). *Pacific Science* 64(1): 23–31. (Documentation of the erythrina gall wasp threat to *Erythrina sandwicensis*.)

[7] U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. (1994). *Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants; Determination of Endangered Status for the Plant Sesbania tomentosa (ʻohai)*. Final Rule, Federal Register 59: 56333–56351.

[8] Hawaii Department of Land and Natural Resources, Division of Forestry and Wildlife. *Naio Thrips (Klambothrips myopori)* — invasive-pest fact sheets and Kauaʻi detections (2009 onwards). https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/hisc/info/invasive-species-profiles/naio-thrips/

[9] Handy, E. S. C. & Handy, E. G., with Pukui, M. K. (1972). *Native Planters in Old Hawaii: Their Life, Lore, and Environment*. Bernice P. Bishop Museum Bulletin 233. Bishop Museum Press, Honolulu. (Canonical ethnobotanical record; source of the Hāloa tradition and kalo cultivar diversity treatment.)

[10] Krauss, B. H. (1993). *Plants in Hawaiian Culture*. University of Hawaiʻi Press, Honolulu. (Standard reference for Hawaiian ethnobotany and canoe-plant uses; used here for kī, noni, kō, ʻaʻaliʻi, māmaki cultural framing.)

[11] Abbott, I. A. (1992). *Lāʻau Hawaiʻi: Traditional Hawaiian Uses of Plants*. Bishop Museum Press, Honolulu. (Second canonical ethnobotany; complementary treatment of canoe plants and cultural context.)

[12] Kamakau, S. M. (1976). *The Works of the People of Old / Na Hana a ka Poʻe Kahiko* (translated by M. K. Pukui, edited by D. B. Barrère). Bernice P. Bishop Museum Special Publication 61. Bishop Museum Press, Honolulu. (Nineteenth-century Hawaiian historian; secondary literature access to pre-contact cultivation traditions.)

[13] National Tropical Botanical Garden. *Meet the Plants — plant profiles*. https://ntbg.org/database/plants/ (Peer-reviewed reference profiles for all six species in this branch.)

[14] Rock, J. F. (1913, reprinted 1974). *The Indigenous Trees of the Hawaiian Islands*. Charles E. Tuttle Company, Rutland, Vermont (reprint of 1913 Honolulu edition). Public domain. (Used for wood-use and traditional-material notes on ʻaʻaliʻi and māmaki. Also cited as branch-b [1] for niu-tree cultural framing; retained here for material-culture claims specific to Sapindaceae/Urticaceae.)

[15] U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. (2021). *Sesbania tomentosa (ʻohai) 5-Year Review: Summary and Evaluation*. Pacific Islands Fish and Wildlife Office, Honolulu. Available at `ecos.fws.gov/docs/five_year_review/doc3334.pdf`. (Current-status document for the federal endangered listing; reports fewer than 700 wild individuals across the main Hawaiian Islands — Kauaʻi, Oʻahu, Molokaʻi, Maui, Hawaiʻi — plus hundreds more on Nihoa and Mokumanamana; confirms Kauaʻi remains within extant range. Superseded reliance on the 1994 listing rule alone; cycle-4 addition per Branch B verification.)

[16] Burney, D. A. et al. — Makauwahi Cave (Kauaʻi) subfossil plant record. National Tropical Botanical Garden field reports; summarised in NTBG "Kou" profile (`ntbg.org/database/plants/detail/cordia-subcordata`). (Subfossil evidence for pre-human presence of *Cordia subcordata* on Kauaʻi, supporting the indigenous status treatment adopted here. Cycle-4 addition; deferred for potential future rewiring into the kou.yaml uncertainty framework — currently not cited from any species page.)
