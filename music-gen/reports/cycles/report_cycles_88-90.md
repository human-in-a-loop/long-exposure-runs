## Introduction

The v4 campaign closed at c77 and was augmented at c78. The v5 arc that follows has a different shape: full-length transcription of the 26-song corpus (MuScriptor chunked full-length, canonicalized to MIDI, six stems plus full_mix per song), a reindex layer to repair an index-collision defect inherited from the c22 merge and the c4 serializer, a tempo-detection criterion sought under pre-registered falsification (each candidate criterion is pre-registered with an enum verdict and a meta-gate that fires after three consecutive falsifications), a reindex-fidelity statistic, first-data rules extraction (groove and harmony Markov models), and a MANDATORY ear virtual environment whose build is disk-gated under the FD-1 90% ceiling. The corpus driver runs detached at roughly 25-27 minutes per song and is the binding schedule constraint; disk is the binding resource constraint.

Cycles 88-90 are v5 cycles 2 through 4 (internal c79-c81). The range delivers the reindex infrastructure, three consecutive pre-registered tempo falsifications that converge on a sharp mechanism, and first-data groove statistics — and it fails to land its own closure at Cycle 90 because the worker turn ended mid-cycle before running the emitter. The auditor's role in this range is unusually load-bearing: the on-disk audit substituted for the missing work_output, the auditor patched the Rome lossy-only landing in place, and the auditor's independent recomputation of v5c is what surfaced the lag-quantization mechanism.

## Discussion

Three things about this range are worth naming.

First, the auditor's independent recomputation is what turned a third falsification into a mechanism. The worker's v5c run was correct, honest, and byte-deterministic; it reported `RULES_OUT_AUTOCORR_DIRECT` with Disco A missed by 0.0062 and did not retune. Under the meta-gate that was enough to stop the criterion search. But the audit did not stop at verifying the numbers — it recomputed them to four decimal places, then looked at the parabolic-refined peaks, and found that Disco A's true period is 21.484 frames, that the integer candidate at 21 evaluates its 2T harmonic one frame short of the real 2-beat peak, and that PD escapes only because its refined period happens to be near-integer. That is a specific, testable mechanism (integer-lag quantization of a fractional true period causing off-peak harmonic reads) with a specific confirmation statistic (refined-lag BPM within ±1 of all five anchors; Disco A's harmonic sum at refined T/2 and 2T exceeding the 80.75 candidate's). The range's value is not that v5c failed; it is that the failure is now explained well enough that the next cycle can test the explanation rather than guess a fourth criterion.

Second, the range's closure failure is a process defect that the on-disk-authoritative discipline was designed to survive, and it did — at real cost. The worker turn ended mid-cycle with a two-line fragment; the emitter and POR registration scripts were written, tested, and never run; Rome landed lossy under an old driver image and a live test failed. The audit substituted a direct on-disk verification for the missing work_output, patched Rome in place with the worker's own tested hook (proving byte-identity across two fresh tempdirs before recording it), recorded the fix with `agent: auditor` so the provenance is honest, and caught its own inherited omission of the `agent` field on the first append. This is the right shape for an auditor fix — minimal, tested, attributed, and disclosed — but it inverts the intended role split, and the ledger/POR half of C-1 remains open. The next cycle's first act is to run the emitter with `agent` added and to skip the Rome event the auditor already landed.

Third, the `agent` field omission is a recurring process defect that survived three cycles because attribution was assumed rather than checked. The c78+ emitter template dropped a required field; the c80 audit claimed "0 c80-attributable" ERRORs; the c81 worker's baseline note repeated it; both were wrong on attribution while right on counts. The audit's correction — check validator attribution by line number, not by assumption — is the generalizable lesson, and the recommended `_infra/emitter-agent-field-c82` disclosure event (do not rewrite history) is the right remedy. The same pattern shows up in the venv-headroom JSON: a correct-looking number measured against the wrong reference frame, propagated one cycle before being caught. Both are the kind of drift that a pre-registered, on-disk-authoritative discipline eventually catches — but "eventually" cost a cycle each.
